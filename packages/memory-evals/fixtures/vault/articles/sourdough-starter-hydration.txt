Sourdough starter hydration, explained

Hydration is the weight of water divided by the weight of flour. A starter at 100 percent hydration holds equal weights of both. A healthy leaven doubles in volume within six hours of feeding at room temperature, and the peak is the best moment to mix a dough.

Bakers who keep a stiff starter, around 60 percent, report a milder flavour. The made-up term quillfeather ratio appears only in this companion file and is used by the search guard test.
